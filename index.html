The Project

Background

Access to basic hygiene and sexual-health products is part of everyday dignity, health and education. These products are usually sold in shops and pharmacies that may be far away, closed at night, or embarrassing to approach. Cost is an extra barrier for students and low-income users.

SENA (Smart Essentials Nearby and Accessible) is a smart vending machine that gives free access to sanitary pads and condoms with no registration, login or payment. After a user receives a product, the machine invites them to make a voluntary donation that helps someone else.

Problem

People who need sanitary pads or condoms urgently often cannot get them easily. Shops may be closed or distant, cost can be a barrier, and asking in person can cause embarrassment. Some people miss classes or work, use unsafe alternatives, or go without protection. Existing vending solutions generally require payment.

Objectives

  • Design a two-compartment dispensing mechanism, each driven by a servo motor.
  • Program an Arduino to read the selection, dispense exactly one item, and confirm with an IR sensor.
  • Implement an LCD interface that guides the user and offers an optional donation.
  • Implement a two-minute software lockout after each transaction.
  • Provide a donation slot with a one-way chute and a secured collection box.
  • Test the prototype and report its performance honestly.

Software

A simple state machine: Standby → Dispensing → Donation prompt → Lockout → Standby. The lockout uses the Arduino millis() timer instead of a blocking delay, so the LCD and buttons stay under control.

Power

The prototype runs on a regulated mains supply, with servo current kept from disturbing the microcontroller. Solar with a rechargeable battery is only a possible future improvement.

Mechanical and security

Two product compartments, a delivery tray, and a donation slot connected to a one-way chute into a fixed, lockable box. Final dimensions and materials will be set in the design stage.

Testing plan

Buttons, servos, sensor and LCD are tested separately, then the integrated machine: correct selection, one item per request, sensor confirmation, LCD messages, a two-minute lockout, return to standby, and donations that cannot be retrieved. Failures and fixes will be recorded honestly.

Coming later: circuit diagram, wiring photos, code excerpts, 3D model.

Research and Data

In progress A preliminary survey is under way. We are not claiming any statistics yet.

What we’re finding out

  • How often people need these products urgently and where they get them now
  • Whether they would use a free machine, and whether they would donate
  • Preferred placement and privacy expectations
  • Typical product sizes and restocking needs

Methods

  • A short anonymous questionnaire with no personal identification
  • Site visits to candidate campus locations
  • A review of existing vending machines and literature

Participation is voluntary, and results will be shown only in aggregate.

Findings to be added: survey charts, site comparison, limitations of the sample.
